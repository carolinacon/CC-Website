CarolinaCon History

Celebrating 20 Years

This October will be the 21st CarolinaCon conference. In honor of this, we're celebrating the 20 YEARS of CarolinaCons that have come before us with some special events and merch!

CarolinaCon's founding vision was to be a place for people, from all walks of life, to come together and learn about technology and security — without the big ticket price of many mainstream conferences.

It's been 20 years of hackers coming together to connect, share, and grow. A community inclusive of all ages, genders, backgrounds, and knowledge-levels.

We are deeply grateful for this wonderful community, including our all-volunteer staff, passionate speakers, and attendees that have kept us going all these years.

A Look Back

While you can find an overview of what CarolinaCon is on our About page, we'll dive more into our history and transitions here.

CarolinaCon Begins

CarolinaCon was the brainchild of a man by the handle Vic Vandal. He had the vision for a conference where anyone could come learn about offensive and defensive security at an affordable price.

Vic had been a co-organizer of two underground hacker conferences in New Orleans, originally started by a man who went by the handle Flex. In the early 2000s, Vic, Flex, and others moved away. The conferences faded out after a couple years.

In 2004, he pitched a conference idea at a Raleigh 2600 meeting. In 2005, the nonprofit "The CarolinaCon Group" was established, and Vic's pitch became a reality with the very first CarolinaCon.

Vic funded the conference with his own money the first few years, even as it lost money, determined to keep the cost low for conference attendees. Tech was rented or borrowed, presentations were on bedsheets or plain walls; the CarolinaCon team did whatever they could to share and learn from others who embodied the hacker mentality.

Eventually, the conference started to make a small profit, which was used to invest in gear needed for the conference. The conference continued to grow for many years, each year with a new theme/web design, cool workshops, and presentations.

Hackers came to learn, share, eat together, team up for the CTFs, and just have a good time with fellow enthusiasts. It became the place to be, especially when we were college students back in the day with little money in our pockets but the desire for a place in the security community.

Who has run CarolinaCon?

CarolinaCon was run by Vic and a volunteer staff out of Raleigh NC from 2005-2018, with the non-profit board making any major decisions. With Vic doing the bulk of the work for many years, he was long overdue for a break. After CarolinaCon 14, he decided it was time to pass the torch.

Vic and the board members discussed their options for who could take over the conference. They collectively decided to ask the 49th Security Division (aka the 49th), who excitedly and appreciatively accepted.

The 49th is an ethical hacking club at UNC Charlotte. At the time, they had been attending the conference each year, bringing large groups of students from the College of Computing and Informatics programs with them. Some of its members had also presented at the conference previously.

While the handoff was announced to everyone, there was some confusion about it. When the 49th hosted their first conference in 2019, Vic provided a longer explanation for clarity with this statement. Current and graduated 49th members continued to host the conference in the following years.

As some of the officers and more active members graduated, the CarolinaCon staff became an all-graduate crew. As their life demands increased, the staff had a budget cuts edition of the conference, ultimately announcing CarolinaCon would be put on hiatus. After this announcement, other 49th alumni reached out to carry the torch. The staff is now down to them, but we all stay connected with each other, bouncing ideas around and bonding over tech. This year we're intent on revitalizing the con in hopes of eventually bring it back in person.

How is CarolinaCon funded?

Since its founding, CarolinaCon has been run by an all-volunteer staff, covering costs with internal and external donations, ticket prices, and merch. All proceeds always go to funding the conferences, the staff never takes a salary.

Online cons have not had all the organization costs of an in-person con, but there are still fees we cover for hosting or running digital things.

If you like CarolinaCon's mission and want to support us, you can help us spread the word online by sharing our content, give us a follow on any of our socials, present at the conference, or donate. If you're curious why we only take donations, not sponsorships, you can read more here.

We greatly appreciate all of you! 💛

What happened to in-person CarolinaCons?

TL;DR: COVID

CarolinaCon (and all of us) took a hit with COVID. What started as postponing one of our conferences turned into a switch to an online format that has kept things alive the last few years.

Having the con online allowed us to keep our costs to run the con low and admission free. It also allowed our staff to work together more remotely as we managed busy lives and the COVID reality.

Will we ever go back to in-person? Yes!

In-person cons are absolutely our goal. We can't wait to see you all again, to hack together in the same room, to get a bite between talks, and just hangout.

The cost to host an in-person con will be the biggest factor. We'll see how merch sales and donations go this year and do all we can to continue to run the con for the lowest admin costs and ticket prices possible.

This year we are excited to celebrate all the years of CarolinaCons, aiming to revive our awesome community of hackers and be back in-person as soon as possible (hopefully next year)! 🤞
